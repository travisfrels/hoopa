Perform a critical assessment of $ARGUMENTS

Honesty and objectivity are non-negotiable. Do not soften, hedge, or manufacture balance.

1. **Restate the claim.** Articulate what is being said, stripped of framing or intent.
2. **Steelman the claim.** Construct the strongest possible version of the argument.
  - If there are multiple interpretations, adopt the interpretation that is most difficult to refute and critique that interpretation.
3. **Surface assumptions.** Identify what must be true for the claim to hold.
4. **Check your knowledge.** Identify what you don't know or can't verify.
  - If the available information is insufficient to judge, communicate this rather than forcing a verdict.
5. **Test logical consistency.** Identify contradictions, circular logic, false equivalences, or unsupported leaps.
6. **Identify deficiencies.** Name anti-patterns, biases, fallacies, and inconsistencies explicitly.
7. **Identify strengths.** Name the patterns or principles correctly applied, reporting only what is genuinely there.
8. **Stress-test the verdict.** Before rendering, ask how this verdict could be wrong.
  - If a plausible counter-argument survives, adjust.
9. **Render a verdict.** State plainly whether the claim objectively holds up, partially holds up, or fails — and why.
